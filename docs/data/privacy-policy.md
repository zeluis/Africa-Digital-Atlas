# Privacy Policy & Academic Legal Terms

## Institutional & Legal Framework

The Africa Digital Atlas operates under an uncompromising sovereign data governance model, strict EU GDPR compliance, absolute client-side data minimization, open-source FAIR data principles, and scholarly institutional independence.

---

## Pillar 1: GDPR & International Privacy Compliance

### Zero-Surveillance & Client-Side Architecture
The Atlas does not deploy advertising trackers, behavioral fingerprinting scripts, third-party pixel beacons, or cross-site tracking telemetry:
- **No Third-Party Cookies**: No tracking cookies or commercial ad network scripts are loaded.
- **Client-Side Storage**: All active user state (selected language, theme preference, reading progress, and orientation history) is stored strictly client-side using `localStorage` on the user's browser instance.
- **Serverless & Transient Audio**: Audio requests via native Web Speech API execute 100% locally on the user's hardware. Neural speech synthesis requests are processed as transient, stateless in-memory streams without retention, logging, or biometric vocal fingerprinting.
- **Right to Erasure (GDPR Art. 17)**: Users can inspect and wipe their local state at any time via the in-app Data Sovereignty utility.

## Pillar 2: Academic Advisory & Institutional Independence

### Explicit Institutional Decoupling
The Africa Digital Atlas is an independent educational and scholarly digital platform. 
Referenced university scholars, economic historians, bioarchaeologists, and institutions (such as Prof. Nathan Nunn, Harvard University, American Economic Review, Quarterly Journal of Economics, the SlaveVoyages Consortium, and UNESCO) are cited strictly for empirical provenance under academic fair use. 

They are not institutional affiliates, sponsors, or commercial partners of the platform unless explicitly stated.

## Pillar 4: Historical Sensitivities & Pedagogical Ethics

### Ethical Representation of Difficult Historical Themes
The platform examines difficult historical themes including transatlantic chattel slavery, colonial extraction, and bioarchaeological human remains. We adhere strictly to UNESCO Memory of the World guidelines and international bioethics treaties to ensure dignified, respectful, and pedagogically sound representation.

All data concerning forced migration and demographic extraction are presented to honor ancestral memory and foster structural, evidence-based policy dialogue across Africa and the global diaspora.
